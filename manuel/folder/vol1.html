Inspecteurs de la librairie refers to a class of inspectors created by royal decree in 1777. Inspectors were obligated to perform rigorous quarterly inspections of every printing shop. The inspectors played a key role in controlling the book trade by reporting their findings directly to the central government, which resulted in the ordered closure of many printing shops.

The garde de sceaux was a distinct royal position held prior to the revolution, whose duties have since been subsumed by the Minister of Justice. The garde des sceaux used the sceau, or official French seal, to validate and authenticate letters patent including documents like pardons.

The lieutenant-général de police was a position established in 1667 that worked closely with the king. Towards the second half of the 18th century, the role was understood as protecting the interests of the rich and opressing vulnerable populations.

It is the editors' best guess that this refers to Paul-Guillaume Simon, the printer of the Parisian parliament from 1741-1782. Reputable biographical information on Simon is scarce.

La Foire de Beaucaire was a large annual market that drew significant international trade within and outside of Europe as a result of its location on the Rhône and its proximity to the Mediterrannean. The market would go on to disappear by 1870 after a lengthy period of decline beginning roughly around the revolution. Vie et mort d'un grand marché international.
