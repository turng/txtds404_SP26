This is an excerpt (page 7-8) from Lonesome Mermaid.

“I’ll remember everything,” I said. “It is a timeless life,” he said dreamingly. “But I got lonesome for my guitar. I’ve asked the queen, that is the mermaid, to get it back. I’m on my way to my house now.” “Your house is not there any more.” I said. “It disappeared years ago.” “Did some man steal it?” he asked. “It just rotted away with the years,” I said. “You have been gone a long time. A war came and killed most of the young menbbefore my generation. Then a plague and a famine came after, killing the rest including my own generation. What you will see here are remnants of a one vigorous race. I don’t know what will happen to us in another generation.” “My heart beats for you,” he said sadly. “But I’ve got to go now. The queen will be disappointed when she hears that my guitar is gone.” “I’ve a guitar,” I said. “I want to give it to you. I want you to be happy with the queen in that timeless place.” “Thank you, son,” he said. “You are very understanding and kind. I’ve sacrificed my life for this town, but that is nothing. If I didn’t go with the queen, she would have taken many of the young men in this town.” “But why would she do that?” I asked. “They wouldn't understand her,” he said, “I would have understood her,” I said. “I’m glad we understand each other,” he said. “I might want to live on the earth again, and you could take my place.” I remained silent. “They wouldn’t have understood her,” he said again. “They are too human.” ‘Thank you for your sacrifice,” I said. “Please give my best regards to the queen.” “Now, remember that mermaid are really kind and understanding,” he said. “They are cruel sometimes because men don’t understand them. Men of the earth are always thinking of happiness in terms of happiness on the earth, Mermaids get lonesome, too.” “I’ll always remember,” I said. “Well, goodbye,” he said. I watched him walked straight into the water. And the day became sud-denly dark and murmurous. I heard the turbulent lashing and roaring of the water in the momentary darkness. And when the sun came out the man was gone, forever. And the mermaid never came out of the secret depths again. Sometimes the townspeople could hear the faraway sound of a guitar rising from the river when the moon was full in the summer sky.
