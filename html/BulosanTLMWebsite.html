The Lonesome Mermaid

Every once in a while the mermaid emerged from the depths of the river when the moon was full and claimed her victims. She would appear among us in the person of a beautiful young woman, then suddenly disappear with her new young man. We often wondered why she always took with her the most promising man in our town. We burned candles and chanted prayers when we knew that the time had come when she would appear again. But nobody actually saw her until a strange man came to live in our town. Before his appearance we only noticed a strange and beautiful woman in our religious processions, then the next day a promising young man with some beauty would disappear forever. But the strange man who came to live in our town finally gave us the reality of the mermaid’s existence and her traffic with promising young men. At first the men did not pay any particular attention to the stranger, because he was always sitting under a mangotree in the yard. He was always playing a homemade guitar. But the women looked out of their windows and sighed with sympathy, because they thought the man was merely waiting for the hour of death. As the years went by, however, the townspeople began to forget him. Then on evening toward the end of November, when we were harvesting the highland rice in the south and or our field, the people began talking ominously about the mermaid. Then my uncle came running with the starling news that she would soon appear to claim her next victim. “And this time, brother,” he vehemently to my father, “her victim is the stranger!” “How do you know?” Father asked.
